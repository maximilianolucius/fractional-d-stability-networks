\section{Discussion and Conclusions}\label{sec:discussion}

The matrix-theoretic and ecological parts of this paper address the same robustness question at two levels. The matrix problem asks when an entire positive-diagonal orbit remains inside the Matignon sector. The ecological problem asks whether that exact orbit geometry can occur at a positive equilibrium of a nonlinear, biologically interpretable system. The answer is affirmative in dimension three, but the route to that answer exposes several distinctions that are easy to blur.

First, the genuinely fractional effect studied here is stronger than ordinary order-dependent stabilization. For a fixed Jacobian it is well known that decreasing the Caputo order can admit spectra that are not Hurwitz \cite{Matignon1996,BrandiburGarrappaKaslik2021}. Our condition requires this angular stability to survive \emph{every} positive diagonal left scaling. In ecological language, the equilibrium remains fractionally stable under arbitrary positive rescaling of component time scales, even though some member of the same orbit fails classical Hurwitz stability. Classical \(D\)-stability already has a substantial ecological robustness literature \cite{ChenWangLiu2024}; the new ingredient is the exact Matignon analogue in dimension three and the explicit geometry of its difference from Cain's half-plane criterion.

Second, dimension three is not merely the first dimension in which fractional stabilization can occur. Fractional-only examples already exist in lower dimension. The exact statement proved here is stronger and more specific: dimension three is the minimum dimension in which the genuinely fractional multiplicative-\(D\)-stable class has nonempty full-dimensional interior. The two-dimensional equality mechanism in Theorem~\ref{thm:2d} is therefore a useful warning against imprecise priority language.

Third, the threshold \(T_\alpha(\beta)\) separates universal diagonal robustness from fixed-polynomial root-location theory. Generalized \(D\)-stability frameworks and forbidden-boundary criteria are known \cite{Kushel2019,KushelPavaniJDDE}; fixed-cubic fractional Routh--Hurwitz criteria are also known \cite{CermakNechvatal2017,BourafaAbdelouahabMoussaoui2020}. What the three-dimensional reduction adds is complete elimination of the multiplier variables. The four invariants in Equation~\eqref{eq:four-invariants} are sufficient to classify the entire orbit, and Theorem~\ref{thm:strict-convexity} turns the remaining threshold into a unique smooth optimization problem. Cain's result is recovered exactly at \(\alpha=1\), rather than only asymptotically or qualitatively.

The ecological realization clarifies the role of interaction signs. Generic statements that feedback loops influence stability are classical \cite{Kinkhabwala2015}. Here the loop coordinate enters an exact all-diagonal inequality. In the competing-consumer architecture, the directed three-cycle contribution has a fixed sign and forces \(\kappa<T_1\); the desired open difference band is impossible regardless of parameter tuning. In the IGP architecture, one directed cycle changes sign, allowing \(L_3<0\) and opening access to \(\kappa>T_1\). This is a structural explanation rather than a post hoc interpretation of a numerical parameter set.

The Double-Allee law then supplies a one-parameter biological path through this exact invariant geometry. The monotonicity result in Equation~\eqref{eq:allee-monotonicity} holds throughout the positive strict-\(P\) coexistence stratum and does not require \(\chi\ge0\). Thus the Allee threshold has a direct matrix-geometric meaning: increasing \(m\) decreases the effective prey self-restoring slope, increases \(t=1/s\), and moves the invariant point monotonically toward and through the Cain surface. When the crossing condition is met, Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} makes the transition unique and transverse. This mechanism is distinct from the familiar use of Allee parameters to trigger equilibrium loss, saddle-node structure, Hopf bifurcation or extinction in predator--prey models \cite{ChaosSolitonsFractals2015DoubleAllee,Tassaddiq2026,FractalFract2026DoubleAllee}. The statement here concerns robust local spectral classification over an entire positive-diagonal orbit.

Several limitations are important. The results are local-equilibrium theorems: membership in \(\mathcal F_\alpha\) is a statement about the linearized Caputo system and does not by itself imply global nonlinear stability. We do not claim a Hopf bifurcation at the Cain crossing, because the crossing is a boundary of classical all-diagonal robustness rather than a nonlinear bifurcation calculation for one fixed system. The positive coexistence equilibrium used in the construction is a smooth local branch; global uniqueness of positive equilibria is neither assumed nor proved. The W2-A parameter set is a constructive, biologically feasible witness chosen for robust margins and readable coexistence scales, not an empirical calibration. Consequently the numerical values in Table~\ref{tab:w2a} should not be interpreted as estimates for a particular ecosystem.

The next mathematical frontier is dimension \(n\ge4\). The projective reduction in Section~\ref{sec:framework} remains available, but the normalized characteristic polynomial already carries \(2^4-4-1=11\) nontrivial principal-minor invariants in dimension four, and fixed-polynomial Matignon boundary geometry is correspondingly more complicated. It is therefore unlikely that the four-invariant simplicity of the present theorem extends verbatim. A natural program is to identify graph classes or invariant submanifolds for which the higher-dimensional orbit problem retains convex or low-dimensional structure.

In summary, we have solved the real three-dimensional positive-diagonal Matignon stability problem on the full-dimensional strict-\(P(-A)\) stratum by reducing the universal diagonal quantifier to the exact threshold
\[
 \kappa<T_\alpha(\beta).
\]
The threshold recovers Cain's classical criterion at \(\alpha=1\), lies strictly above it for \(\alpha<1\), has a unique smooth optimizer, and yields the minimum full-dimensional robust dimension \(n=3\). We then showed that the resulting fractional-only band is realized by an open set of a nonlinear Caputo double-Allee intraguild-predation model, while a natural competing-consumer architecture is structurally excluded. Finally, the Allee threshold itself generates a monotone path that crosses Cain's boundary transversally into the fractional-only region. The resulting picture links classical \(D\)-stability, fractional spectral geometry and ecological feedback structure through one exact low-dimensional invariant framework.
