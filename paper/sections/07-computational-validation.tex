\section{Computational certification and reproducibility}
\label{sec:computation}

The results in Sections~\ref{sec:exact-3x3}--\ref{sec:threshold-geometry} are analytic. Finite sampling of positive diagonal multipliers cannot prove D-stability; computation is used here only for independent falsification, interval certification of representative thresholds, sensitivity checks, and reproducible illustrations.

\subsection{Independent numerical routes}

Two computational routes were kept separate. The invariant-space route evaluates
\[
T_\alpha(\beta)
=
\min_{x\in\Delta_2^\circ}
\frac{h_\alpha(B_\beta(x))}{x_1x_2x_3}
\]
using logit coordinates, analytic derivatives, high-precision refinement, and interval enclosures. The direct route instead minimizes the spectral Matignon margin
\[
\min_{D\succ0}
\left[
\min_{\lambda\in\sigma(DA)}
|\arg\lambda|-\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}
\right]
\]
over two log-diagonal ratios using eigenvalues of the full matrix \(DA\). Agreement between these routes tests the elimination theorem rather than merely retesting one implementation of the same formula.

The first-stage adversarial campaign generated matrices directly in entry space and invariant space, perturbed the cyclic family, and concentrated samples near the exact fractional boundary, the Cain boundary, principal-minor boundaries, both order limits, and extreme anisotropy. All \(56{,}234\) numerically delicate cases were recomputed with multiprecision arithmetic. No genuine disagreement with Theorem~\ref{thm:C10} survived.

\subsection{Certified threshold atlas}

A second campaign constructed an atlas of \(938\) threshold values covering symmetric, two-equal, antagonistic, same-sign, ecological, and highly anisotropic invariant triples, with orders extending to
\[
\alpha=2/3+10^{-8}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\alpha=1-10^{-10}.
\]
Of these, \(903\) were globally certified by an interval branch-and-bound route that does not invoke Theorem~\ref{thm:strict-convexity}. For \(35\) extreme-anisotropy cases, that route exhausted its box/time budget and the enclosure was completed using the already-proved global convexity and uniqueness theorem. The worst reported relative enclosure width was
\[
1.1\times10^{-35},
\]
and every independent 50-digit optimizer lay inside its reported interval. On the \(312\) fully symmetric atlas points, the closed form in Corollary~\ref{cor:symmetric-slice} lay inside every enclosure.

\subsection{Canonical matrices and robustness}

The canonical library contains \(35\) exact real matrices spanning the main qualitative regimes; ten have interval-certified \(T_\alpha\). Table~\ref{tab:canonical-witnesses} shows four representative examples.

The W22 matrix is especially useful conceptually:
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-3/2&0\\
-1/2&-1&-3\\
-3&0&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is Hurwitz at \(D=I\), with
\[
\max\Re\sigma(A)\approx-0.0789,
\]
but it is not Hurwitz D-stable. The Cain-minimizing diagonal, normalized to unit geometric mean,
\[
D_H\approx
\operatorname{diag}(1.58740,1.58740,0.396850),
\]
gives
\[
\max\Re\sigma(D_HA)\approx0.0384>0.
\]
Nevertheless, at \(\alpha=0.7\) the entire positive diagonal orbit is Matignon-stable by Theorem~\ref{thm:C10}. This separates ordinary stability of \(A\) from orbit-level D-stability.

Robustness was explored for fourteen witnesses using local sensitivity, constrained optimization, global differential evolution, directional root finding, and high-precision refinement. The computations were reported as upper bounds unless a rigorous lower bound was available. For example, the near-fractional-boundary witness W28 has a high-precision max-entry perturbation of approximately
\[
1.06\times10^{-3}
\]
that reaches the fractional boundary. For several low-order witnesses, numerical searches encountered the strict-P boundary before the fractional boundary, showing that the relevant nearest boundary of the robust class need not be the Matignon threshold itself.

\subsection{Differential checks of the threshold geometry}

The C-15 differential structure was tested at \(8{,}000\) points spanning symmetric, two-equal, generic, strongly anisotropic, and both limiting-order regimes. This produced \(128{,}000\) floating-point checks. Near \(\alpha=2/3\), finite differences lose accuracy because
\[
T_\alpha=O(K^{-3});
\]
the \(500\) worst points were recomputed at 40 digits, producing \(2{,}500\) high-precision checks. The primary identity
\[
\frac{\partial T_\alpha}{\partial\beta_{ij}}
=
\frac{h_\alpha'(B^*)}{x_k^*}
\]
agreed to at worst \(2.4\times10^{-33}\) in the high-precision rechecks. The logit Hessian was positive definite at every tested point; its smallest observed eigenvalue was \(4.6\times10^{-3}\).

\subsection{Code and data availability}

The computational record is maintained at
\[
\text{\url{https://github.com/maximilianolucius/fractional-d-stability-networks}}.
\]
Reusable routines are in \path{src/fdsn/}; reproduction scripts are in \path{computations/scripts/}; compact numerical outputs and interval certificates are in \path{computations/results/}. The repository records fixed random seeds, software versions, testing conditions, and exact commands for the reported campaigns. A snapshot of the submission commit will be archived with the supplementary materials.

Computation remains logically separate from proof: the numerical campaign was designed to falsify or certify representative calculations, not to infer the theorems from sampling frequency.
