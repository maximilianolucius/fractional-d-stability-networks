\section{Dimension two: exact obstruction}

Let
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact \(2\times2\) classification]
For every \(0<\alpha<1\),
\[
A\in\mathcal F_\alpha^{(2)}
\iff
\det A>0,\qquad a\le0,\qquad d\le0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(D=\operatorname{diag}(x,y)\succ0\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(DA)=xa+yd,
\qquad
\det(DA)=xy\det A.
\]
If \(\det A\le0\), a zero or positive real eigenvalue occurs. If \(a>0\), keeping \(x>0\) fixed and letting \(y\downarrow0\) produces a persistent positive eigenvalue near \(xa\); similarly \(d>0\) is impossible. Conversely, \(\det A>0\) together with \(a,d\le0\) gives nonpositive trace for every \(D\). The eigenvalues are either both negative real or a complex-conjugate pair with nonpositive real part, hence their argument magnitudes are at least \(\pi/2>\alpha\pi/2\).
\end{proof}

Classical Hurwitz D-stability additionally requires that \(a\) and \(d\) are not both zero. Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_\alpha^{(2)}
=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&b\\
c&0
\end{pmatrix}
:bc<0
\right\}.
\]

\begin{corollary}
For every \(0<\alpha<1\),
\[
\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^4}\mathcal P_\alpha^{(2)}
=
\varnothing.
\]
\end{corollary}

Thus dimension two can exhibit fractional-only D-stability, but only on a lower-dimensional boundary set. A robust full-dimensional separation from classical D-stability cannot occur before dimension three.
