\section{Positive-Diagonal Matignon Framework}\label{sec:framework}

Fix \(0<\alpha<1\) and set
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_\alpha
 :=
 \left\{
 z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:
 |\arg z|>\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
For real \(n\times n\) matrices define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_\alpha^{(n)}
 :=
 \left\{
 A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:
 \spec(DA)\subset\Sigma_\alpha
 \ \text{for every positive diagonal }D
 \right\},
 \label{eq:Falpha}
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_H^{(n)}
 :=
 \left\{
 A:
 DA\text{ is Hurwitz for every positive diagonal }D
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The genuinely fractional difference class is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_\alpha^{(n)}
 :=
 \mathcal F_\alpha^{(n)}
 \setminus
 \mathcal D_H^{(n)}.
 \label{eq:Palpha}
\end{equation}
The terminology ``\(D\)-stability'' is used here exclusively in the multiplicative matrix sense of Equations~\eqref{eq:Falpha}--\eqref{eq:Palpha}, not in the distinct control-theoretic sense of locating polynomial roots inside a prescribed \(D\)-region.

The Matignon criterion for a commensurate Caputo linear system states that asymptotic stability is equivalent to spectral containment in \(\Sigma_\alpha\) \cite{Matignon1996,BrandiburGarrappaKaslik2021}. Thus \(A\in\mathcal F_\alpha^{(n)}\) means that every member of the positive-diagonal left orbit of \(A\) is Matignon stable.

\subsection{Principal-Minor Orbit Coordinates}

Suppose \(-A\) is a strict \(P\)-matrix. Write
\[
 p_i=-a_{ii}>0,
\]
and for each nonempty index set \(I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\),
\[
 m_I:=(-1)^{|I|}\det A[I]>0.
\]
For \(D=\diag(d_1,\dots,d_n)\succ0\),
\[
 \det(\lambda I-DA)
 =
 \lambda^n+c_1(D)\lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(D),
\]
with
\begin{equation}
 c_k(D)
 =
 \sum_{|I|=k}
 m_I\prod_{i\in I}d_i.
 \label{eq:coeff-principal}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:coeff-principal} is the standard principal-minor representation of characteristic coefficients; its use in feedback-cycle interpretations is classical \cite{Kinkhabwala2015}.

Normalize
\begin{equation}
 x_i
 =
 \frac{p_id_i}{\sum_jp_jd_j},
 \qquad
 x_i>0,
 \qquad
 \sum_ix_i=1.
 \label{eq:simplex-normalization}
\end{equation}
The positive-diagonal cone modulo common scalar multiplication is therefore exactly the open simplex. For \(|I|\ge2\) define the left-diagonal invariants
\begin{equation}
 \beta_I
 =
 \frac{m_I}{\prod_{i\in I}p_i}.
 \label{eq:general-beta}
\end{equation}
After scaling the spectral variable by \(a_D:=\sum_i p_id_i\), the normalized characteristic polynomial is
\begin{equation}
 z^n+z^{n-1}+B_2(x)z^{n-2}+\cdots+B_n(x),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 B_k(x)
 =
 \sum_{|I|=k}
 \beta_I\prod_{i\in I}x_i.
\end{equation}
There are \(2^n-n-1\) nontrivial invariants. We use this normalization only as a structural lemma; no novelty is claimed for the principal-minor identity or the projective simplex reduction.

In dimension three the four invariants are
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{12}=\frac{m_{12}}{p_1p_2},
 \qquad
 \beta_{13}=\frac{m_{13}}{p_1p_3},
 \qquad
 \beta_{23}=\frac{m_{23}}{p_2p_3},
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{-\det A}{p_1p_2p_3}.
 \label{eq:four-invariants}
\end{equation}
They are unchanged by any positive left-diagonal scaling. These coordinates will turn the universal multiplier condition into an exact two-variable threshold problem.
