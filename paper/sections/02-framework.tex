\section{Positive-diagonal Matignon stability and orbit reduction}

For \(0<\alpha<1\), define
\[
\Sigma_\alpha
=
\{z\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}:|\arg z|>\alpha\pi/2\}.
\]

\begin{definition}
For \(A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\), define
\[
\mathcal F_\alpha^{(n)}
=
\{A:\sigma(DA)\subset\Sigma_\alpha\ \text{for every positive diagonal }D\},
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal D_H^{(n)}
=
\{A:DA\ \text{is Hurwitz for every positive diagonal }D\}.
\]
The genuinely fractional difference class is
\[
\mathcal P_\alpha^{(n)}
=
\mathcal F_\alpha^{(n)}
\setminus
\mathcal D_H^{(n)}.
\]
\end{definition}

The definition is an instance of generalized multiplicative region D-stability \citep{Kushel2019,KushelPavani2022}. The distinctive feature here is that \(\Sigma_\alpha\) is a reflex sector: for \(\alpha<1\) it contains a nonempty right-half-plane sliver.

\subsection{Standard orbit normalization}

Assume \(-A\) is a strict P-matrix and define, for nonempty \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\),
\[
m_I=(-1)^{|I|}\det A[I]>0,
\qquad
p_i=m_{\{i\}}=-a_{ii}>0.
\]
For \(|I|\ge2\), set
\[
\beta_I
=
\frac{m_I}{\prod_{i\in I}p_i}.
\]

Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\succ0\) and write
\[
\det(\lambda I-DA)
=
\lambda^n+c_1(D)\lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(D).
\]
The characteristic-coefficient/principal-minor identity gives
\[
c_k(D)
=
\sum_{|I|=k}m_I\prod_{i\in I}d_i.
\]
With
\[
s=c_1(D)=\sum_i p_id_i,
\qquad
x_i=\frac{p_id_i}{s},
\]
we have \(x\in\Delta_{n-1}^\circ\), and every point of the open simplex is realized by a positive diagonal \(D\), uniquely modulo a common positive scalar. Therefore
\[
\frac{c_k(D)}{s^k}
=
B_k(x)
:=
\sum_{|I|=k}\beta_I\prod_{i\in I}x_i.
\]
After the radial rescaling \(\lambda=sz\), which leaves eigenvalue arguments unchanged, the entire positive-diagonal orbit is represented by
\[
z^n+z^{n-1}+B_2(x)z^{n-2}+\cdots+B_n(x).
\]
This standard projective reduction is structural machinery rather than a novelty claim. Its role is to isolate the genuinely dimension-specific root-location problem.

For \(n=3\), the nontrivial orbit data reduce to four invariants:
\[
\beta_{12},\quad\beta_{13},\quad\beta_{23},\quad
\kappa=\frac{-\det A}{p_1p_2p_3}.
\]
