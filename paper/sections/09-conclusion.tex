\section{Conclusion}

We obtained an explicit exact solution of the positive-diagonal Matignon generalized-D-stability problem for real \(3\times3\) matrices on the full-dimensional strict-\(P(-A)\) stratum. The universal diagonal multiplier is eliminated in favor of four orbit invariants and a scalar threshold \(T_\alpha(\beta)\). The threshold is a strict fractional deformation of Cain's classical real-\(3\times3\) D-stability boundary, is generated by a globally strictly convex two-dimensional problem, and has sharp limiting behavior at both the classical-order and low-order transitions.

The resulting genuinely fractional band is full dimensional, while dimension two admits only a lower-dimensional separation from classical Hurwitz D-stability. Consequently, dimension three is the first dimension in which robust purely fractional positive-diagonal stability occurs. For three-species generalized Lotka--Volterra systems, the same theory yields abundance-invariant feedback-loop coordinates and exact sensitivity relations.

The general positive-diagonal orbit reduction extends to arbitrary dimension, but the dimension-specific root geometry becomes substantially harder from \(n=4\) onward. This provides a natural next problem without weakening the exact closure obtained here in dimension three.
